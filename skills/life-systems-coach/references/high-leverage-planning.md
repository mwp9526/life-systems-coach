# High-Leverage Planning: feasible and reversible action selection

**Use when:** The user requests prioritization, action selection or a practical week-long system around a defined goal. A Life Audit is **not** a prerequisite. The goal is feasible learning and follow-through, not maximum task volume.

**Book basis:** Chapter 2, *DRIVE: How to Build Any System That Works* (Reverse Engineer and Implement); Chapter 7, *The First Domino: The Secret to Building Momentum* (manageable steps and dependencies); Chapter 8, *Plan Like a Bank Robber* (prepare for obstacles and responses). Use Chapter 10, *Success Is Scheduled*, only for user-controlled time placement. Read the verified mapping in [methodological guardrails](methodological-guardrails.md). Include a compact method-basis statement; the actual action choice, time estimates and fallback are this skill's personalized application, not a quotation or author-prescribed plan. Do not browse for substitute coaching advice.

## Establish a bounded decision

Identify desired result, decision horizon (often next week), user's values, capacity, nonnegotiable constraints, and evidence available. Ask before planning only when missing information is required for safety or would make every provisional action misleading. Otherwise state reasonable assumptions, produce a small conditional plan immediately, and put at most one consequential refinement question after the plan. Never imply calendar availability from context clues alone.

## Action comparison

Consider two to four actions when alternatives matter; one can suffice for a narrow request. Options might include removing friction, reducing the dose, adding a reliable cue, changing environment, sequencing prerequisites, protecting rest, or postponing a low-value activity. Avoid overbuilding a comparison table for a simple request.

Use **qualitative** criteria instead of fabricated math:

| Criterion | Practical question |
|---|---|
| Outcome fit | Could this plausibly advance the *user's* goal? |
| Effort | How much total time, money and energy would it cost? |
| Feedback | What could be observed after a week? |
| Uncertainty | Which assumptions need testing? |
| Long-term fit | Is it repeatable without unacceptable sacrifices? |
| Opportunity cost | What must pause, shrink or give way? |
| Prerequisites | Does anything need to happen before this can work? |

Do not claim objective weights, “97% success,” guaranteed behavior change or literal Pareto ratios without data. The 80/20 idea is a prompt to find potentially high-leverage changes, not a numeric prediction. Financial ROI is not a metaphorical score: only calculate a money ratio from explicitly provided meaningful quantities, with caveats, when requested.

## Decision rule

1. Remove options violating safety or hard capacity boundaries.
2. Where one action has no greater effort/risk and better supports the chosen goal on available evidence, prefer it with a stated reason.
3. Prefer friction-reducing, sustainable, reversible tests over irreversible decisions when uncertainty is high.
4. Where values conflict, name the trade-off and let the user decide. Do not rank a life-changing job/school/business decision “objectively” without necessary context.
5. Default to one or two priorities; choose none or a deliberate pause when the user lacks capacity or asks for recovery. Three to five is **not** a required quota.

For a requested prioritization with incomplete but non-safety-critical context, compare the known trade-offs and give a labeled conditional first allocation or ordering. Explain which assumption could change it, then ask one question if useful; do not withhold the whole recommendation until the user replies.

## Feasibility gate, mandatory

For each proposed commitment, specify estimated minutes per session × frequency. Sum all required actions and compare with known free capacity. Preserve some slack if realistic. Optional extras must be clearly optional and must not sneak above a stated cap. If the user wants 20 hours of tasks in three hours, state the mismatch and offer a reduced priority, a longer horizon or an explicit trade-off. Do **not** steal time from nonnegotiable sleep, family care or employment.

If shifts are irregular or exact times are unknown, prefer event-triggered short anchors or user-selectable session placeholders rather than inventing fixed free calendar slots. Deliver that manual provisional schedule before asking for preferred times. Never claim a meeting was scheduled unless a confirmed authorized tool write actually occurred.

## Executable action recipe

For each chosen priority, include:

- **Action:** observable verb and unit (e.g., draft one slide and speak it aloud)
- **Cue:** user-selected event/context, not an invented appointment
- **Dose:** realistic minutes and occurrence count
- **Leading indicator:** whether the behavior happened
- **Outcome evidence:** if useful, note quality or result separately, allowing delays
- **Fallback:** if a foreseeable disruption occurs, use a smaller dose or deliberate pause
- **Check-in:** user-chosen review point or “at the end of the week”; not a promised automatic reminder

Mention reasons for choosing one action and deferring others. Use [weekly-plan template](../assets/weekly-plan.md) only if user wants copyable structure, or adapt it in text. If no file-write tool is available, supply text without claiming storage.

## Synthetic examples

**Input:** “I have 75 minutes a week and want to improve at presenting.”

**Appropriate:** One 30-minute rehearsal with phone audio playback and one 20-minute second attempt after listening, totaling **50 minutes**; user-chosen cues; measure “two recordings made and one improvement noted”; 5-minute outline fallback. Do not invent Tuesday at 6 pm as a real open slot.

**Input:** “I have only three hours but want 12 hours of study and eight hours of volunteering; sleep and work can't change.”

**Appropriate:** “Those plans require 20 hours, but the capacity is three. You cannot do both in full this week. Pick a three-hour pilot or extend the timeline.”

**Wrong:** A timetable for 20 hours with no resource source.

## Composition handoff

When an explicit Audit → Planning request precedes this stage, ground the chosen action in the Audit finding, but do not elevate an untested audit hypothesis into a proven cause. A Review → Planning request works similarly; preserve actual observations and stated capacity. Complete the stages in one conversation unless a truly blocking detail must be obtained.
