# Life Audit: diagnostic exploration without compulsory scoring

**Use when:** The user wants to understand where to focus, why a pattern repeats, or how a life concern connects to practical constraints. This is one of three primary workflows, not required onboarding for planning. Follow universal rules in `SKILL.md`.

**Book basis:** Chapter 2, *DRIVE: How to Build Any System That Works* (Define), and Chapter 3, *The Life Engine Audit* (desired/current-state gap and choosing a focus). Read the verified mapping in [methodological guardrails](methodological-guardrails.md). Explain the relevant book method briefly in the answer; the selective questions and small experiment below are this skill's original adaptation. Do not substitute internet advice for this method.

## Input sufficiency

Minimum input: a concern/desired direction or an explicit broad-audit request. Useful optional information: what has been tried, an example of the recurring obstacle, available capacity, values, environment, dependencies and nonnegotiable obligations.

If the only request is “audit my life,” do **not** administer a seven-domain questionnaire by default. Ask one bounded question (“Is there one area to focus on, or do you want a brief whole-life scan?”) or offer a short, opt-in domain outline. Respect refusal to share details. A narrow concern typically requires only one or two relevant domains; other domains are permitted if relevant.

## Procedure

1. **Frame:** Rephrase the user's concern neutrally, without implying that they have failed or need to maximize every life domain.
2. **Scope:** Identify the few relevant domains from the user's words (e.g. schedule, relationships, health routines, work or environment); do not assume fixed seven-domain ratings are useful.
3. **Baseline evidence:** Record what is *reported* about actual actions/conditions. Treat unspecified history and outcomes as unknown.
4. **Constraints and levers:** Identify only relevant time, money, energy, obligations, environment, access, attention and control constraints. Distinguish controllable changes from external pressures.
5. **Friction:** Compare intention versus available opportunity. Consider competing priorities, timing/cues, task size, complexity, support, resources and goal fit. Label causal explanations as **hypotheses** until checked.
6. **Focus:** Offer one defensible focus in light of importance, likely reach, reversibility, control and cost. Where values are in tension, show an alternative and invite choice.
7. **Next experiment:** Propose one small reversible observation or action, or ask the single question that would change the conclusion most. Only run a full Planning stage when explicitly asked.

## Response structure

Use plain paragraphs or a compact table when that helps:

- **What you want:** the user's direction, not a new goal supplied by the agent
- **What is known:** observations / user report (with evidence)
- **What is uncertain:** possible contributors, stated as hypotheses
- **Constraints:** material resource limits
- **Suggested focus:** why this focus rather than alternatives, with agency preserved
- **Next step:** a small test or single material question

In a quick audit omit labels that add no information. For a broader audit, invite user-defined areas rather than replicating the book's original worksheet.

## Worked synthetic micro-example

**Input:** “I keep skipping language practice after late shifts. I have one hour a week and want to work out why.”

**Suitable output:** “What you reported: sessions scheduled after late shifts are often missed; you have roughly 60 minutes weekly. A plausible, *unverified* reason is that the after-shift cue depends on energy you may not have. The first focus is placement rather than raising the target. To test it, try a short session linked to a more reliable transition on one day you choose, or tell me which time is actually predictable.”

**Wrong:** “You lack discipline; all seven of your life areas need 1–10 ratings.”

## Composition handoff

If user explicitly requested Audit → Planning, forward the *stated* objective, factual constraints, observations, untested hypotheses and recommended focus into [High-Leverage Planning](high-leverage-planning.md). In the final answer, show the Audit finding before the plan, state what was assumed, and preserve time limits. Do not request already supplied capacity again. This does not create a fourth workflow.
